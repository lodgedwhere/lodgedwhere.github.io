# Learning is reentry

In bare distinction, the first mark creates a difference. Reentry occurs when the distinction is taken as given and used to generate further distinctions within the marked space.

Learning often has exactly this structure.  
At first, a proposition appears as an object of attention, eg   
“The Earth orbits the Sun” or   
“Addition is associative.”  
These are explicit contents of thought. They are marks being examined.

But once *learned*, they cease to be objects and become conditions constraining further thought. They are no longer questioned at every step. They become part of the background structure from which new distinctions are drawn.

The learned proposition is ***reentered;*** it functions as an axiom.

You can see this in mathematics very clearly. A student initially struggles with the axioms of arithmetic. Later they become invisible. The mathematician does not continually prove that 2+2=4; arithmetic has been reentered as a space within which further activity occurs.

The same thing happens psychologically.  
A child learns:  
“I am separate from others.”  
This “fact” gradually becomes a tacit assumption. Eventually it is so deeply reentered that experience itself is organized around it.

The assumption disappears from awareness precisely because it has become structural.  
This is why deep learning often feels less like acquiring information and more like becoming unable to see alternatives.  
Every successful learning process creates blindness as well as capability.  
The new axiom expands what can be done within the space while simultaneously constraining what can be imagined outside it.

In a framework suggested by Spencer-Brown’s Laws of Form, this becomes especially interesting:  
\- Learning = incorporation of distinctions into the implicit structure of the system.  
\- Thinking = manipulation of distinctions within that structure.  
\- Insight = noticing one of the hidden distinctions that had become structural.  
\- Awakening = recognizing the distinction that grounds the entire structure.

The last case is unusual because the distinction being examined is not a proposition among propositions.  
It is the distinction that makes propositions possible.

This may be why spiritual traditions repeatedly describe realization as *unlearning* rather than learning.  
Nothing new is acquired.  
Rather, a deeply reentered axiom is finally seen as an axiom.

The ego says:  
“I have learned something.”  
Awareness notices:  
“A distinction has become implicit.”  
And awakening notices:  
“Even the distinction that says ‘I’ is implicit.”
